Chapter one · Block 1

Home: the route, the examples and the methods

Block 1 is the app’s home page. It computes nothing: on a single screen it says what PostHarvestPro does, sums up the route of a product from harvest to the consumer, presents the eight working blocks, opens the ten example data sets in the tool that matches each one, lists the methods with their source, explains in plain words the physiology behind the computations and gives the program’s citation. It is the place you come back to when starting something new or to remember where everything is.

1.1The home page

When you open index.html, the app starts in Block 1. Unlike the other blocks, this one has no tabs and no data cards: it is a presentation page that you scroll from top to bottom, and the In this block list on the right leads to each of its sections.

At the top is the presentation panel. A small label states the subject of the app, Postharvest physiology and technology; below it comes the motto, Measure quality, predict shelf life and decide with data, and a paragraph that lists what it does, from quality indices and color to the packaging and storage calculators, and ends with two promises: every result states its method and its source, and the data never leave your computer.

  • 1Start with an example → Opens Block 2›Quality summary and loads the example mango into it: the fastest way to see the app with data (Chapter 2).
  • 2See the example data. Scrolls down to the Example data section of the same page (Section 1.4).
  • 3Methods and sources. Scrolls down to the list of methods with their citation (Section 1.5).
  • 4How to cite. Scrolls down to the program’s citation, at the end of the home page (Section 1.8).
  • 5The four badges. Runs entirely in your browser, Validated against independent implementations, Spanish and English and SVG figures and 300 ppi PNG. They are only labels; they cannot be clicked.
  • 6The illustration. A fruit in five stages of ripeness—Mature green, Breaker, Turning, Ripe and Overripe—over the firmness of three lots stored at 13, 20 and 25 °C, with the band of the market limit and the day the 20 and 25 °C curves cross it.

The illustration has its caption: Simulated: firmness falls with first-order kinetics; the warmer the store, the sooner shelf life ends. It is a drawing, not a result: it does not change with the data you load. But it sums up the central idea of the program, the one Chapter 3 turns into numbers: the same fruit, with the same initial firmness, reaches the limit on very different days depending on the temperature at which it is stored. The curves are drawn from left to right when the home page opens; if you turn animations off with the sparkles button in the top bar, or if your system asks for reduced motion, they appear already drawn.

The three buttons below only scroll the page. The first one, on the other hand, does the same as the Open in 2 · Quality summary button on the mango card in Example data: it changes block and tab, loads the table and shows the results.

iThe home page is never cleared

Loading an example or your own data changes nothing in Block 1. What does change is the bar on the left: the number of a block turns into a check mark when that block already has a result. The PostHarvestPro name in the top bar always brings you back here.

1.7What it does not do

Four boxes say plainly what cannot be expected of the program. They are the same limits that Section I.10 of the introduction tabulates, which adds two more (it does not simulate package dynamics or read instruments):

  • It does not replace measurement. The models summarize data; extrapolating far from the temperatures, times or cultivars measured is the user's responsibility, and the app warns when it happens. The most visible warning is in Block 3›Temperature, which flags as an extrapolation any storage temperature outside the measured ones.
  • It brings no trade standards. The tables of sizes, permeabilities and compatibility are orientative and editable: use those of your standard, your film and your cultivar. The size-grade templates in Chapter 2 are an example of this.
  • It does not model non-isothermal kinetics. Predictions are at constant temperature; variable temperature histories need k(T(t)) integrated over time.
  • It uploads nothing. There is no server, account or connection: what you save is a project file on your computer.

1.8References and how to cite

The last two sections of the home page are the bibliography and the citation. In References, the See the 81 references panel opens the complete list of the works cited by the methods, in alphabetical order; it is the same list that Appendix F gathers, there grouped by topic.

How to cite begins with If PostHarvestPro helps your work, please cite it as: and gives the program’s citation in two forms, each with its button:

  • The reference, in the language of the interface, copied with Copy the citation:
    Barrera-Guzmán, L. Á. (2026). PostHarvestPro: postharvest physiology and technology, quality, shelf life and sensory evaluation in the browser (Version 1.0.0) [Computer software]. https://luisangelbg.github.io/PostHarvestPro/
  • The BibTeX entry, with the key barreraguzman2026postharvestpro, the English title, version 1.0.0, the year, the license GPL-3.0-or-later and the address; it is copied with Copy BibTeX.

When you copy, a brief notice confirms Copied. The citation does not yet carry a digital object identifier (DOI): it will be added when the program is published with one. The same citation is at the end of Block 9 (Section 9.7) and at the end of every report. The home page closes with a footnote holding the User’s manual link, which opens this manual in another tab (with the interface in Spanish it reads Manual de usuario (en español) and opens the Spanish edition), and a line saying that PostHarvestPro 1.0.0 is free software (GPL-3.0) and part of the LABG Suite.

✓Where to start
If it is the first time you open the appthen press Start with an example → and go through Chapter 2 with the mango; then open the same mango in Block 3 (Open in 3 · Kinetics).
If you have measurements of a lot over timethen start in Block 2 to summarize them and go on to Block 3 for their shelf life and to Block 7 to compare the treatments.
If your question is about respiration, losses, tasting or packagingthen go straight to its block (4, 5, 6 or 8): each one works with its own table or with its typed fields.
If you do not know what columns your table should havethen download the ⤓ CSV of the most similar example and use it as a template.
If you are going to publish the resultsthen cite the program from here or from Block 9, and also cite the method of each result.

They are guides for finding your way, not a mandatory order.

✕When something goes wrong

An Open in … button takes me to the tab, but I see no results. The results are below the data input; scroll down or use the In this block list. The copy buttons do nothing. Some browsers do not allow the clipboard on pages opened as a local file; select the citation text and copy it by hand, or open the app with Open PostHarvestPro.bat (Section I.5). The illustration is not animated. Animations are turned off (sparkles button in the top bar) or your system asks for reduced motion; it does not affect any computation. I lost what I had loaded when I opened an example. The example replaces the tool’s table; if you saved the project, open it again in Block 9.
